# Certainty

We're new to developing software using AI. Autocomplete based on machine
learning has a history going back about 10 years, but only became popular
sometime around 2022. The term "vibe-coding" was coined in March 2025, naming
something people were experimenting with before (I vibe-coded a Rust-based web
app in 2024). Agentic AI first started to appear in 2023 with tools like Aider,
but widespread adoption only started in 2025, still ongoing -- the future is
distributed unevenly.

It's a massive change in many ways. I'd like to highlight the emotional , social
and psychological impacts on software developers, even developers who don't use
it are affected. Some of our skills we built up over years are likely less
valuable. Signals of commitment and thought we previously had have dimished in
clarity. Collaboration, so important in software development, is affected.

We're all grappling with this change. We are exploring how to integrate LLMs in
our workflows, and when human attention is most valuable. I'd say there is a lot
that is unclear right now, and the situation is still changing rapidly.

In this I find it interesting that we have so many people who seem to have a
certainty already about the best way to go forward. I have for instance seen
multiple confident declarations that code quality is dead. 

When I remarked the other day on the psychological impact of AI on developers,
which is a topic I think we should explore, it got a lot of positive traction,
but on the other hand I was told by quite a few people that I'm holding the tool
wrong. Sometimes the intent behind that is helpful, but I also sensed a
dismissive tone. These are people who apparently have it all figured out.

Certainty is useful: it makes you feel more at ease. Certainty is also socially
beneficial: if you confidently declare that you know the way to go, people tend
to listen to you. People look at you as a person who has it all figured out.

But certainty in software development is rarely a good place to be: it's good to
be aware of practices but their application is context-dependent and should be
considered carefully. Good engineering is about trade-offs. Software engineering
as a field itself is relatively young and it keeps changing. The study of how
well technical practices work is famously difficult.

We have had always people who confidently declare they know how to do it. Such
people spread ideas, which is good. But reality often tends to be a bit more
complicated.

It's especially absurd that a little over a year in the agentic AI coding
takeoff we have people confidently declare that they're doing it right. It makes
me feel insecure because I am not confident; I'm busy learning, adapting,
retuning my intuitions. I have thoughts and inclinations; I am betting that to
sit down and engage with the code is still of value in many contexts. But I know
that there are many contexts too where that may not matter so much. 

I'm sure that this projected certainty makes others feel insecure as well. So
I'm here to say that you should not let this certainty distract you from
learning. Think. Take your time.
